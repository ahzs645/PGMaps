# BC road costs: consultant estimates, contracts and completed projects

**Research date: 13 November 2026 · Prepared for PGMaps**

## Findings

Public project records add useful construction-cost evidence beyond supplier price lists and appraisal allowances. This package contains **14 disclosed contracts**, **14 crossing-project cost records**, and **26 selected unit rates from five consultant estimates**. It also preserves source discrepancies and seven newer or additional tender leads that have not been confirmed against a primary award record.

The most useful evidence has either an itemized work schedule or enough physical scope to compare similar projects. A project total with no length or quantities can still document an awarded contract, but cannot support a road-kilometre rate. All amounts retain their historical cost basis; none has been relabelled as a current 2026 price.

## 3. A useful breakdown: Freeman Creek bridge

The BC Fish Passage Program report supplies a complete multiyear breakdown for a 12 m bridge near Yahk, completed in September 2020.

| Cost group | Reported CAD |
|---|---:|
| Fabrication, 2018 | $80,400 |
| Installation contract, 2020 | $82,000 |
| Habitat assessment, environmental plan, survey and design, 2018 | $12,300 |
| Engineering and foreman supervision, 2020 | $10,800 |
| Environmental monitoring, 2020 | $10,800 |
| Administration, 2020 | $19,630 |
| **Multiyear total** | **$215,930** |

The rows reconcile. The annual summary's $123,230 is the 2020 portion, not the full project. This is a useful model for showing what a bridge cost includes: structure, installation, professional work, environmental work and administration. It is not a reusable multiplier between fabrication and installed cost.

Source: [BC Fish Passage Program Annual Report 2020–2021](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/fish-fish-habitat/fish-passage/fish-passage/bcts_fish_passage_annual_report_2020-2021.pdf), printed page 7. Tax treatment is unspecified. The wider CWF Freeman Creek and Meadow profile covers two bridges and must not be substituted for this single-bridge record.

### McElhanney — Strathcona Area D drainage study, 10 January 2025

The road-restoration schedule estimates **225 mm granular base at $40/m²** and **300 mm granular subbase at $50/m²**. Its quantities assume a 2.5 m wide asphalt cut along storm mains. These are utility-trench restoration components. The 40% contingency is separate, and the staff summary excludes engineering, legal, consulting and permitting costs. PVC storm-main prices are not forestry-culvert prices.

Source: [SRD Area D drainage study](https://agenda.strathconard.ca/SRDAttachments/SRDCommittees/Open/EAS/11-Mar-25/20250304-Area-D-Stormwater-Mgmt-Study.pdf), Appendix B, PDF page 21; staff financial implications page 3.

### BGC — Jason Creek / Reid Road, report dated 18 February 2022

The diversion-berm estimate includes **excavation $6/m³**, **offsite sediment disposal $16/m³**, **berm fill supplied and placed $34/m³**, and **Class 3 riprap supplied and placed $137/m³**. Contractor general costs, contingency and engineering/permitting are separate allowances.

The underlying prices mix 2015–2017 Alberta averages, an April 2020 Alberta bid and North Vancouver crossing bids. BGC states uncertainty of −50% to greater than +100%. They are useful scope examples but cannot be labelled current BC regional market prices. The access-construction row also lacks a defined road width/standard.

Source: [BGC assessment in the SLRD RFP bundle](https://www.slrd.bc.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/RFP%20Reid%20Road%20Area%20Detailed%20QRA%20Final.pdf), Appendix B, printed B-1 / PDF page 62.

### Urban Systems — Delkatla Spit, Masset, 20 October 2023

This waterfront recreation concept contains clearing at **$30/m²** and a **100 mm crushed-rock pedestrian surface at $150/m²**. Its base estimate includes buildings, utilities, shoreline landscaping and amenities. These rows are retained as remote-island site context, with `context_only` status; neither is suitable as a forestry-road aggregate default. The 2024 council-agenda date is not the price year.

Source: [Masset council agenda, 19 August 2024](https://massetbc.com/images/uploads/pdfs/August_19,_2024_Agenda_Package.pdf), PDF pages 10–11.

## 5. Proposed use in PGMaps

Add **Project comparisons** alongside appraisal and material references. For a selected region, show the work type, cost status, original cost year, amount, dimensions/quantities, scope, source and quality flags. Keep contract/project totals separate from unit-rate observations. An estimate or maximum contract amount should never receive a final-cost badge.

For Cariboo, a construction contract and a deactivation project can provide useful evidence even while a local supplier's material price remains quote-only. These records support different questions. The contractor's total should not replace the supplier's gravel price or the Interior subgrade allowance.

Suggested comparison workflow:

1. Match new construction, upgrade, maintenance, crossing replacement or deactivation.
2. Match geometry, loading, ground conditions, access, traffic accommodation and environmental work.
3. Compare the same cost boundary: direct construction, installed component, or full project.
4. Retain original cost year, taxes, allowances and missing fields.
5. Show evidence without changing the project estimate. Any future rate adoption should be an explicit user action.

Natural-resource regions are provisional geographic tags in this research. Hixon/Tako is deliberately unresolved, and MoTI's Cariboo District must not be mistaken for the natural-resource region. An exact corridor still needs a spatial boundary check and its own appraisal applicability review.

## 6. Data quality and remaining gaps

The package distinguishes source-confirmed facts from inference and unverified discovery leads. Known discrepancies include Parksville's summary subtotals, Loveland's $2.91 component-versus-total difference, and conflicting Birk project amounts. Burman and Gollen remain in the source-review file. Original figures have been retained with flags.

Seven secondary tender leads are supplied separately for follow-up. They are not verified award evidence and have not been turned into unit prices. The primary awarded-contract examples recovered here are historical 2020–2021 records; there is no primary current 2026 accepted forestry-road bill of quantities in this package. Northeast operational-road construction remains a gap.

Annual accounts and grants have distinct uses. McBride's 2024 statements, for example, separately report road construction, rehabilitation, maintenance and capitalized road acquisitions. Those accounting categories cannot be divided by a year's kilometres without reconciling the cost/quantity boundary. FESBC approved funding is also not an invoice or final construction cost. See the context file for these additional records and their primary URLs.

## Deliverable and checks

The accompanying JSON files contain complete selected records, source locators, dates, cost categories, tax bases, allowances and limitations. Validation checks unique identifiers, positive amounts, unit-rate extensions, Freeman's component sum, the Smithers amendment and preservation of known discrepancies. No broad regional price or automatic estimate update was produced.
