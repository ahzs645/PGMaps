Ministry of Forests

(UPDATE) Flood Watch – Nechako River
ISSUED: 9:15 AM July 19, 2022

The River Forecast Centre is maintaining a Flood Watch for:

      Nechako River

Delayed snowmelt from cooler spring temperatures and ongoing wetter than normal conditions
in the spring and early summer led to sustained high flows on the Nechako River and its
tributaries. Hydrometric stations on the Nechako River have reached their highest levels of the
season this week. The upcoming weather forecast predicts very warm and dry conditions for
the next seven days. Flows are expected to stabilize and begin receding through the rest of
summer. Since river levels are very high for this time of year, the region remains at risk if an
unseasonal heavy to extreme rainfall event occurs in the following few weeks.

A summary of river conditions as of 8:30AM PDT, today:

      The Nechako River at Isle Pierre (08JC002) is flowing at 858 m3/s (between 5 and 10‐year return
       period flow). It reached the highest level of the season on Sunday July 17th at 864 m3/s.

      The Nechako River at Vanderhoof (08JC001) is flowing at 529 m3/s (between 5 and 10‐year
       return period flow). It reached the highest level of the season on Sunday July 17th at 532 m3/s.

      The Nechako River below Cheslatta Falls (08JA017) is flowing at the highest level of the season
       at approximately 412 m3/s (between 10 and 20‐year return period flow).

      The Nautley River near Fort Fraser (08JB003) is flowing at 102 m3/s. The river reached its highest
       level this season of approximately 109 m3/s on July 12th (2‐year return period flow).

      The Stuart River near Fort St. James (08JE001) is flowing at 331 m3/s. It reached the highest level
       of the season on July 9th of approximately 345 m3/s.

The public is advised to stay clear of the fast‐flowing rivers and potentially unstable riverbanks
during the high‐streamflow period. Be prepared and know your hazards.

The River Forecast Centre continues to monitor the conditions and will provide updates as
conditions warrant.

A High Streamflow Advisory means that river levels are rising or expected to rise rapidly, but that no major flooding

is expected. Minor flooding in low-lying areas is possible.

A Flood Watch means that river levels are rising and will approach or may exceed bankfull. Flooding of areas

adjacent to affected rivers may occur.

A Flood Warning means that river levels have exceeded bankfull or will exceed bankfull imminently, and that

flooding of areas adjacent to the rivers affected will result.
