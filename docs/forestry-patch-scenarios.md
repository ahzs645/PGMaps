# Block planning and Prince George forest patch scenarios

Open **Forestry tools → Block planning** at `/dev/forestry/block-planning`. The planner answers one question: if a block is cut now, later, in another shape or not at all, how does the shared landscape's young-forest patch distribution move against the 2004 Prince George order's Objective C targets over the next fifty years? An empty plan opens on step 1 with **Open the sample** (a synthetic teaching landscape) and **Open a plan file**.

## The plan

A plan (`pgmaps-block-plan/v2`, `src/pages/block-planning/plan.ts`) has five parts, and only the last two are opinions:

| Part      | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Area      | Historic forest district and natural disturbance sub-unit (the Objective C reporting unit), the first projection year, the inventory's source and date, and the plan, tenure and order versions relied on |
| Baseline  | Past harvests and stand-replacing disturbances every schedule shares, each with a kind, a year and whether that year came from the source, was entered, or was assumed                                    |
| Blocks    | Each proposed block: a name and one or more shapes. A second shape is a reconfigured version of the same block                                                                                            |
| Schedules | When each schedule cuts each block, or that it does not, and which shape. A schedule can follow another moved by a number of years; its own entries override the followed schedule block by block         |
| Reviews   | Per block, the rule packs chosen and the evidence entered; per block and schedule, each stream crossing with its own review; and the landscape's D.5 rationale draft                                   |

One schedule is marked as the proposal under review; new blocks start in it at the first projection year. Every edit is a function in `edits.ts`, so each interface does exactly the same thing. Files written by the planner before blocks and schedules were separate (`pgmaps-patches-v1`, `pgmaps-block-plan/v1`) open through `migrate.ts`, which turns reference footprints into the baseline, merges a polygon drawn identically in the proposed and alternative schedules into one block, and says what it changed.

## The five steps

1. **Area.** Choose the district and sub-unit (Table 7's targets show under the choice), the first year, and the baseline. **Unit boundary and DataBC** reads the unit and the past disturbances (below); a past disturbance can also be drawn, or imported as GeoJSON polygons with a `year` property (a missing year is assumed five years before the start and marked).
2. **Blocks.** Draw a block with the shared map sketch (click corners; click the first corner or double-click to finish; Backspace takes a corner back) or import GeoJSON. Select a block for its gross area, its shapes, **Draw another shape**, and its overlap with loaded riparian zones.
3. **Schedules.** Add a schedule that follows another moved by some years, a copy to edit, or one that cuts nothing. The grid gives each block's year per schedule; a blank in a following schedule reads through (shown in italics), **Not cut here** overrides it and **Follow again** clears the override.
4. **Compare.** Every schedule's class shares at the year on the map, each class marked closer to or farther from its target since the period before, and how many periods each schedule moved the distribution as a whole farther from Table 7 and moved any one class away. Where the Forest Practices Board published the reporting unit's 2015 and 2018 distributions (FPB/IRC/235, Appendix 4; `packages/legal-rules/observations/`), they are tabled with each class's movement and drawn in the charts as their own series, never joined to the projection: the two years used different inventories and 2018 the revised 2017 guidance. The **Planning index** ranks the schedules on measured indicators (distance from the targets, periods with a class moving away, area cut in the first ten years and over the horizon), each scaled 0 to 1 across the schedules and weighted, in Index Lab's manner. It is marked as not a legal test everywhere it prints and feeds no review, pathway or finding. **Explain a figure** opens the patch contributions and the field-date priorities for one schedule; both use the same calculation table and formula as Index Lab.
5. **Review.** Two parts. The Prince George order is reviewed once for the landscape: the patch trend is measured from the schedule under review over a chosen five-year interval (**The young-forest trend**, below), and the D.5(b) (rationale, submitted) and D.5(a) (recruitment strategy, submitted and approved by the minister's designate) pathways are checklists whose determinations are the reviewer's answers, never the model's. Every other instrument is reviewed per block; a new block starts with FPPR 1.2.0, the cutting and road permit pack (FRPA §§3, 10, 15.1, FPPR §§14, 34.2, 34.5 and the Forest Act's permit and special purpose area sections), the Omineca old growth and sensitive area orders, the visual quality orders and the Mackenzie biodiversity order (`forestry-profile.json`), each of which asks first whether it applies, and `bc.block-crossings`, whose one question is whether the block changes a stream; each stream crossing the reviewer lists is reviewed on its own (**Stream crossings, one at a time**). *Mackenzie old forest* in the same step calculates that order's targets for one landscape unit or group and shows the margins under its questions as evidence, never as answers ([the old-forest contract](forestry-old-forest.md), **The Mackenzie order**). Questions come from the evaluator's trace (`review/questions.ts`): a settled rule asks nothing, and an open rule asks the first unknown of each open alternative, so an empty FPPR case asks one question and each answer unlocks the next. Findings read in words and open the rule flowchart. A finding's citation opens the provision itself (see **The law behind a finding**), and each provision group has a **Decision flowchart** of its instrument. [Crown grazing and reforestation](forestry-crown-grazing.md) has its own review for the selected block and schedule: whether the block can be cut given that the stand must come back. Releases are chosen per case; installing others happens on `/dev/legal-rules`.

### The young-forest trend

Objective C reads "Demonstrate a trend toward the young forest patch size distribution", and Table 7 states that distribution as each size class's percent of young forest. From `bc.pgtsa.biodiversity` 1.3.0 (`bc-pgtsa-planning-1.3.0.json`; 1.2.0 and the 1.1.0 and 1.0.0 releases are kept for reviews saved with them) the rule `distribution-trend` reads the distribution as a whole: its distance from the targets is the four classes' distances added, in percentage points of young forest (`distributionDistance`, `src/pages/forest-patches/legalEvidence.ts`), and the rule raises when that distance grew from the start of the interval to its end (`patch-distance-growth` > 0, the operation's `distance-growth`). Half the distance is the least share of young forest that would have to sit in another class to match Table 7, so every class counts at its own size and none is weighted above another; a class whose target is 0 counts its whole share. It is the measure the planning index and the Board comparison already add (and the package's advisory `patchDistributionDistance`), and the order offers nothing that would weight one class or one large gap more. A change within 1e-8 points is no change. A period without young forest, with unresolved size classes, or with shares that do not add to 100 makes the interval unknown: the rule then waits on evidence, never reads as not triggered.

Releases to 1.2.0 raised the finding when any one class moved farther from its target (`patch-class-away`). That is stricter than the words: on the Board's 2015 and 2018 distributions it raises McGregor Plateau and Wet Trench – Mountain, which came 11.5 and 47.5 points closer as a whole (`legalEvidence.test.ts`). The class test is kept as evidence: the 1.3.0 fact's evidence names the classes that moved away and by how much, the period table and the printed summary keep **A class moved away** beside **Distance grew (points)**, the assessment record keeps `movedAway` beside `distanceGrowth`, and the patch explanation and field priorities still explain and rank by class. Both the forest patches adapter (`patchFacts`) and the landscape review (`measuredTrend`, `trendMeasurements` in `review/pathways.ts`) read `distributionTrend`, and the review hands both outputs over, so a review saved on 1.2.0 reads its own test and opens unchanged; new reviews start on 1.3.0 (`forestry-profile.json`). The interval is the planner's five-year period: the order sets none, and whether a trend is shown over several periods stays the reviewer's.

What is still open is listed, in order, in [Block planner: next steps](forestry-block-planning-next-steps.md).

## The law behind a finding

Every citation in the review opens the provision in a dialog (`ProvisionLink`, `src/pages/legal-workbench/ProvisionPreview.tsx`): the section as BC Laws prints it, the paragraphs the citation names outlined, the words the rule quotes marked, and a link to the section's own anchor on BC Laws (`…/14_2004#section64`). An order's provision shows its transcribed quotes and opens the signed PDF at its page. Nothing loads until a dialog opens; each source is its own chunk.

- **Stored text.** `scripts/legal-rules/import_bclaws.py --source <id>` reads a catalogued statute or regulation from CiviX XML into `packages/legal-rules/catalogue/generated/<id>.sections.json`: every section as numbered blocks (subsections, paragraphs, definitions, tables), its hierarchy, whether it is repealed, and a hash of its text. Run it again for a new consolidation; the diff shows the sections that changed. Never edit those files by hand.
- **Orders.** Orders are PDFs, often scanned, so each is a transcription in `catalogue/orders/<id>.json` (`orderText.ts`): each provision's id as the order numbers it, its page, and quotes of at most 124 characters copied word for word. The signed PDF stays the authority, one click away.
- **Quotes.** `catalogue/rule-quotes.json` gives every shipped rule the words it rests on, by instrument and rule id. `npx tsx scripts/legal-rules/rule-quotes.ts` seeds a rule without one from the paragraph its provision cites, and never replaces a quote a reviewer chose. `src/lib/legal/ruleQuotes.test.ts` fails when a rule has no quote, when the stored text no longer prints a quote word for word (the signal to read the rule again after a new consolidation), and when any citation in a pack or the ground catalogue names a section that does not exist, is repealed, or has no such paragraph.
- **Citations.** `src/lib/legal/provisions.ts` reads the provision strings the packs use ("64(1)(b) and 64(2)–(4)", "s. 45(1)(b), (c)", "1 and 3, with FPPR 44(3)(d)", "I, Part C.2–C.4") into sections and paragraphs. It reads numbers only and interprets nothing.

The **Decision flowchart** (`DecisionFlowchart.tsx`, logic in `src/lib/legal/decisionTree.ts`) draws an instrument's rules a topic at a time as one tree: each rule's scope and trigger become a route of questions in the pack's question order, routes asking the same question first share it, branches are labelled with the answers the rules test, and each route ends at its rule with its provision. The case's answers light the route they take; an unanswered question stops it (dashed). Selecting a rule shows its finding beside the provision text. It copies as Mermaid or saves as SVG. The evaluator decides every finding: `decisionTree.test.ts` checks, on random answers to every shipped pack, that the tree reaches exactly the rules the evaluator raises.

**Law coverage** on `/dev/legal-rules` (`CoverageMap.tsx`, `src/lib/legal/coverage.ts`) maps every section of the stored statutes and regulations and every provision of the transcribed orders: whether a pack rule or a ground screen cites it (read from the packs and the ground catalogue, so it cannot drift) and what a section-by-section reading found (`catalogue/section-review/<id>.json`: relevance to a block, whether the rules really test it, the gap and the questions a rule would ask). The reading is machine-assisted and dated, never a legal review. **Every gap, all sources** lists the high and medium gaps, and the CSV carries all of it. When a rule comes to cite a section, its review entry is updated to say what the rule still leaves to the reviewer.

Every section in force and every order provision also carries **topics**: one to four labels from one shared vocabulary (`catalogue/topics.json`: 42 labels in eight groups, such as *Stream crossings and culverts*, *Drainage and water control*, *Streams, lakes and wetlands*, *Reforestation and free growing*, *Range, grazing and hay*), most central first. The labels are the same for every Act, regulation and order, so choosing a topic on the coverage map gathers every provision on it from all of them (FPPR §§55 and 72–82, the Water Sustainability Act and Regulation, FRPA, the orders), and the coverage book has a **By topic** index. A label says what a provision is about, never that it applies to a block. A new label goes in `topics.json` before it is used; `coverage.test.ts` fails on a label not in it, a section in force without one, and a stored source without a review.

Each order and policy in the catalogue (`catalogue/sources.json`) also records **what it is made under** (`enabling`: the stored sections, such as Land Act s. 93.4, GAR s. 7(2) or GAR ss. 9, 10 and 12, with a text note for an authority PGMaps does not store, such as the Forest Practices Code Act or the Operational and Site Planning Regulation) and **the natural resource districts it covers** (`areas`, by the codes of the `/dev/boundaries` natural resource layers, named in `catalogue/admin-areas.json`). An order's districts come from its own polygons on DataBC where they are mapped (legal objective plans, winter ranges, habitat areas, landscape units), each district holding at least 1% of the order's area, and otherwise from the district the order names, checked by the district a town in it falls in; `basis` says which, with the read date. A district says an order applies somewhere in it, never that it reaches a block: the map layers decide that. On the coverage map a section's dialog lists the orders made under it, an order's map states what it is made under and where it applies, and **Natural resource district** lists every order and policy that covers a district; the coverage book has the same table and a by-district list. `coverage.test.ts` fails on an order without areas or without what it is made under, an unknown district, and an enabling section that is not stored or is repealed.

The **coverage book** ([`docs/legal-coverage-book.md`](legal-coverage-book.md)) is the same review compiled for reading or printing outside PGMaps: a summary by source, the open gaps, every shipped rule by instrument with what it cites, and each source's sections with their status. `npm run legal:coverage-book` writes it from the review and the packs; `src/lib/legal/coverageBook.test.ts` fails when it is out of date.

### Exceptions

Most rules the law sets have exceptions, and a pack lists them on the rule they except (`exceptions`, **Add an instrument** in `packages/legal-rules/FRAMEWORK.md`), each with its own provision and quote (`rule-quotes.json` under `<rule>#<exception>`). There are two kinds: one that **holds by law** once its facts do (FPPR §64(3)'s 40% basal area, §68(2)'s authorized burn), and one that **needs a decision** by someone the pack names (an approved FSP result under §12.4 or §12.5, a §4.51 exemption, a district manager's COFA variance, an official satisfied of a §215(2) road).

On a raised finding the review offers **Rely on an exception** (`ExceptionClaim.tsx`): the rule's exceptions in a dropdown, then a rationale, a row for each piece of evidence the exception asks for plus one of the reviewer's own, and, for a decision, who decided, when and its reference. The claim is kept beside the answers in the case's fact set (`claims`, `setClaim` in `caseEdits.ts`), one per rule, never as a fact and never chosen for the reviewer. The evaluator keeps the finding raised and reports the claim's standing beside it: *Exception relied on*, *Exception: to answer* (its own conditions wait on an answer, which the topic then asks), *Exception not supported* (the answers contradict them), *Exception pending a decision* (who decided and when are not both recorded) or *Exception decided*. The row's badge shows the standing; the release line counts the rules relying on an exception; the decision flowchart lists each rule's exceptions with their provisions; the printed summary has an **Exceptions relied on** section and the assessment record carries every claim. `exceptions.test.ts` pins that a claim never changes which rules are raised.

**Tenure records** (Review → *Tenure records*, `review/tenureSignals.ts`, `TenureRecords.tsx`) are the block's own public records, read on the reviewer's click and kept for the session only: the Forest Tenures harvesting authorities and cut blocks and the RESULTS openings sharing ground with the footprint, each part read by its own ring, a record touching the block by less than `MIN_RECORD_OVERLAP_HA` (100 m²) left out and counted, and a layer that failed listed as not read. Four entries bear on an exception a rule lists, and each is offered beside that exception on the raised rule (`ExceptionClaim.tsx`): a harvesting authority marked catastrophic or salvage (`CATASTROPHIC_IND`, `SALVAGE_TYPE_CODE`) on FPPR §64(2)'s `salvage-or-natural`, a cutting permit's issue date on FRPA §3(1.1)'s `earlier-permit`, and a cut block marked SP exempt (`SP_EXEMPT_IND`) or an opening in RESULTS' `SPEX` category on FPPR §33(1)'s `fppr-33-cutblock`. Each says what the record says and never why, and is never a fact or a claim: only the reviewer's click relies on the exception and adds the record's words to its *Other evidence*, and the finding stays raised. No holder's or client's field is requested or kept (`keepFields`).

### Review dates and release windows

Each release covers the dates its stored texts were read for (`coverage`), and the evaluator assesses none of its rules on a date outside them (`outside_source_window`). The review never counts those as not applying: `findingCounts` keeps them apart (`outsideDates`), `dateGaps` and `dateGapText` (`review/questions.ts`) name the release, its window and which side of it the date falls, the review shows a notice under the date and in the release, lists the rules as *Not assessed: outside the release's dates* in the open rather than among the settled ones, a crossing whose releases do not cover its date stands *Not assessed on this date* (`undated`), never *Nothing raised*, and the printed summary has **Releases that do not cover the review date**. The old growth, visual quality, Mackenzie and crown grazing releases first covered only 17 November 2026; old growth 1.2.0, visual quality 1.1.0, Mackenzie 1.1.0 and crown grazing 1.3.0 change nothing but their window, which now runs to 27 November 2026, the date their stored texts were read and their orders transcribed (the earlier releases are kept). A historical date, such as a harvested block's permit issue date, stays uncovered: a review then needs the law as it stood then, its consolidation and orders on that date, which no release holds.

### Land status

What land a block is on decides which law reaches its harvest, so `bc.land-status` 1.1.0 (1.0.0 kept) is the first release of every block review (`blockReleases`). Its rules read the stored texts: FRPA's forest practices reach Crown forest land and private land in a tree farm licence, community forest agreement or woodlot licence area, and nothing else (FRPA s. 1, "forest practice"; a First Nations woodland licence is not named, which the rule says is a reading of the text); a woodlot licence holder is governed by the WLPPR and not the FPPR, except its s. 1.2 (WLPPR s. 2(1), (3); FPPR s. 1, "agreement holder"); private managed forest land outside those three licence areas is under the Private Managed Forest Land Act (ss. 1, 3, 45); and on private land, timber reserved to the government is Crown timber (Forest Act s. 1), the boundaries beside Crown land are ascertained before cutting (FRPA s. 53), and the timber is marked and scaled (Forest Act ss. 84, 85, 94, each with the minister's exemption as a `decision` exception). A reserve and treaty settlement land are asked apart and are outside every rule: the Indian Act, a land code and the final agreements are not stored, and no rule is given them until they are.

Release 1.1.0 asks who harvests a block in an area-based licence's area. A woodlot licence, community forest agreement and First Nations woodland licence give their holder the exclusive right to harvest timber on the Crown land in the area, and a tree farm licence the exclusive right to harvest from its area (Forest Act ss. 45(1)(c), 43.3(1)(c)(i), 43.55(1)(c)(i), 35(1)(e)). A harvest by anyone else there (`woodlot-holder` false, or `area-holder-harvests` false) raises `woodlot-exclusive`, `community-forest-exclusive`, `fnwl-exclusive` or `tfl-exclusive`, with the exceptions the Act makes: a BC Timber Sales licence entered into on rights the holder released (s. 22.2(5)), a community forest's reservation for timber sale licences and §47.6(3) forestry licences to cut (s. 43.3(1)(g.2)), a reduction for BCTS licences (ss. 43.3(1)(g.3), 45(1)(f.2)), and another provision of the Act the licence was granted under, a decision by whoever granted it, since each right is "subject to" the Act. The back-test's forestry licences to cut on a woodlot and a community forest fell out of both permit packs because FRPA s. 3's FSP duty is only an agreement holder's, and a minor licence to cut is not an agreement; that is the law, and these rules are what was missing. The land question now says that Crown land "does not include land owned by an agent of the government" (Forest Act s. 1), so land a Crown agency owns is private land here.

The answers are the reviewer's. *On this ground* now reads the two context layers, Indian reserves and treaty lands, whole and keeps them apart from the instruments (`GroundRead.context`, catalogue 1.5.0). From catalogue 1.6.0 a ParcelMap BC parcel typed *Crown Agency* counts as private land, and one typed *Unclassified* or *Mixed Ownership* is its own instrument, *Parcel with no settled owner type*, whose cells are all *Unknown*: while one covers more than a sliver of the block, or from a read made before 1.6.0, no land is offered and the note says why. From a current ground read, `review/landCandidates.ts` offers a candidate for what land the block is on (private parcels, reserves and treaty land by their share of the footprint, whole from `WHOLE_SHARE_PERCENT`) and which licence area holds it (the one covering most of it); **Use** answers the question and records what it rests on as the answer's evidence (`LandOffers.tsx`). A layer not read offers nothing, a read made before the context layers were read offers no land, a block partly on a reserve or treaty land offers no land and says why, and nothing mapped is offered only as Crown land with the reminder that ParcelMap BC is a record, not the title. Whether the land is private managed forest land, whether timber is reserved and whether it is beside Crown land are not mapped publicly, and the hints say where they are.

The pack never removes another release from the review: a finding such as *FRPA's forest practices do not reach this private land* says that the block's FPPR, permit and forest operations findings are not the land's, and the reviewer reads them so. A candidate overlap under `SLIVER_HA` (100 m²) is read as two drawn boundaries meeting and named in a note, never offered as land of that kind: the back-test of 27 November 2026 found private parcels touching harvested blocks by 1–6 m².

Two packs follow from the answer, each also in every block review and asking nothing once the land says it does not apply:

- **`bc.private-managed-forest` 1.0.0.** Shares the land, managed forest land and licence area questions with land status (`sameAs`), so a Crown block is settled without a question. On private managed forest land outside the three licence areas it raises the Council Regulation's practice requirements (BC Reg 182/2007): roads and logging trails held to the minimum (ss. 13–14, which state no figure, so the measured road area is evidence and never a pass or fail), no road within 30 m of a class A or B stream or 10 m of a class C, D or E stream (s. 16, its three exceptions listed), crossings (s. 17), sediment (s. 15), drinking water and licensed intakes (ss. 14.1, 20(2)), tree retention by stream class (ss. 27–29: 30, 25 and 15 trees each side of every 100 m) and understory (s. 30), reforestation at the Interior's 600 or the Coast's 400 crop trees a hectare within 5 years and a regenerated stand within 15 (s. 31), and critical wildlife habitat (PMFL Regulation ss. 5, 7, 8). Section 3's three exemptions are listed on every practice rule. Stream classes are asked by channel width; whether a stream is a fish stream is the reviewer's, and the Council Matters Regulation's definitions are stored.
- **`bc.mineral-tenure` 1.0.0.** A mineral or placer title over the block raises the recorded holder's surface rights and how a later surface disposition stands against them (Mineral Tenure Act ss. 14(1), 16(3); the Act does not say whether a harvest is such a disposition, and the finding says so); where the holder cuts for its own mining, the Mines Act permit first (s. 14(2)) and a free use permit or occupant licence to cut for the Crown timber (s. 14(3)–(4); Forest Act ss. 47.5, 48, 49). A pit or quarry dug for road sand, gravel or rock is a mine under the Mines Act and needs a s. 10 permit unless a regulation or the chief permitting officer exempts it; those regulations and the Health, Safety and Reclamation Code are not stored, so which pits are exempt is not assessed. The ground read offers whether a tenure lies over the block, from the mapped mineral and placer tenures; the block's roads are not read.

The WLPPR's own practice requirements are the next step (`docs/forestry-block-planning-next-steps.md`).

### Stream crossings, one at a time

A block's stream crossings are kept in `Plan.crossings` (`src/pages/block-planning/crossings/record.ts`), each with its own review over the crossing releases (`crossingReleases` in `forestry-profile.json`: `bc.water-crossings` 2.0.0 and `bc.fppr-crossings` 1.0.0), so a crossing with no authority is never hidden behind one that has one.

- **The record.** A crossing has its own stable key (`crossing-<n>`, never reused after a removal, and never a PSCIS or modelled crossing id: PSCIS gave one site two ids 4–12 m apart), a label, the block and schedule, the shape and footprint signature it was listed for, an origin (`entered`, or `designer`, `mapped` or `pscis` when it was added from a read, below), a role (the block's own road, its access road, or not stated), an optional structure (kind, span or equivalent diameter, length, open bottom), an optional place (a point, a road and a stream, each by name or reference), a note, an evidence list, the ids sources give the place (`aliases`), its dated sightings (`sightings`), and its review case. It is kept per block and schedule, like the grazing reviews, and the review step shows the schedule under review.
- **Edits.** Every change goes through `edits.ts`: `addCrossing`, `updateCrossing` (a blank label keeps the old one; a structure, place or note cleared is removed, never stored empty), `setCrossingReview`, `confirmCrossing` and `removeCrossing`; removing a block or schedule removes its crossings. When the block's shape or schedule changes, its crossings are shown out of date with their answers kept, until the reviewer keeps each for the shape as it stands (**Keep it for this shape**).
- **Reading.** Parsed by hand and tolerantly: a plan without crossings opens and writes exactly as before; a field that does not read is left off its record, and a record without its identity or review, naming a block or schedule the plan no longer has, or repeating a key, is left out and counted in the notes on opening.
- **Evaluation and the roll-up** (`crossings/standing.ts`). The evaluator runs once per crossing, over its own releases and fact sets alone. A crossing stands *Needs review* (a rule raised), *Needs an answer* (a rule waits on one), *Nothing raised*, *No release chosen* or *Could not be evaluated*. The block's roll-up is derived every time and never stored: the worst standing across its crossings, each crossing at it listed by its label with its raised findings by title. Exceptions are claimed per crossing and kept in that crossing's own fact set; a claim never clears its finding.
- **Display.** Review → *Stream crossings* (`crossings/CrossingsSection.tsx`) shows the roll-up, a table of the block's crossings (origin, road, structure, questions to answer, raised findings, standing), **Add crossing**, *Find crossings* (below), and the selected crossing's record, what was read about it (its ids, evidence, what that offers for the answers, and its dating) and its review through the same `CaseReview` the block review uses, with the decision flowchart per release. The block's own review leaves the crossing instruments out and asks its one question under `bc.block-crossings`. The printed summary has a **Stream crossings** section (each block and schedule's roll-up, then each crossing with its findings), its **Exceptions relied on** lists crossing claims by block and crossing, and the assessment record carries `crossings`: per block and schedule, the roll-up and every crossing with its assessment.
- **Migration** (`migrateCrossings`, `migrate.ts`). A block review that answered `bc.water-crossings` 1.0.0 opens with that answer set as one crossing, "All crossings, answered for the block before crossings were listed" (`origin: 'entered'`, role not stated, under the schedule under review), kept on release 1.0.0 with its answers and claims; the block review uses `bc.block-crossings` 1.0.0 in its place, with the block's answer to whether it changes a stream carried over (the same question, asked of the block). A 1.0.0 release chosen with nothing answered is only replaced. The notes say what changed, and opening the plan again changes nothing.
- **Kept apart.** Crossings never enter `usePlanAnalysis`, the patch calculation or any check's key (`crossings.test.ts` pins it).

#### Crossings read from the road designer and the map

Review → *Stream crossings* → *Find crossings* (`crossings/CrossingReads.tsx`) reads the places a crossing may be, on the reviewer's click, and keeps each read with the plan (`Plan.crossingReads`, `crossings/candidates.ts`): one per block, schedule and source (`designer` or `mapped`), keyed to the footprint it was read for like the ground reads. A read for the block's earlier shape is refused when it arrives (`saveCrossingRead`), and one kept from before a change is shown out of date and offers nothing to add. What a read found is a list of **candidates**; a candidate becomes a crossing record only on the reviewer's click.

- **From the road designer** (`crossings/designerImport.ts`). The designer's Save & export → GeoJSON writes its crossing register (`buildCrossingRegister`) as one point a row (`registerExportCrossings`), through the shared, DOM-free `src/lib/forestry/designerCrossings.ts` that both tools import, so neither imports the other: the road and its station, mapped, modelled or both, the structure and its sizing (a culvert's diameter and length, a crossing record's structure), what keeps it from counting as sized, the stream's riparian class and its review status, a modelled flow line's catchment, and each mapped crossing's skew and straight approaches. Only the open road's drainage check is known to be current, so only it lists modelled crossings. The stream assessment's assessor never travels. The planner reads the file in the browser (`readDesignerCrossings`; a point without `register_format` is passed over), keeps the rows within `DESIGNER_REACH_M` (20 km) of the block, nearest first, a row inside the footprint on the block's own road and any other with its road not stated, and adds one as origin `designer` with the row's summary as its `designer` evidence.
- **From the map** (`crossings/mappedRead.ts`, live from DataBC, `fetch` and the JSTS overlays passed in). The corridor is stated: every road within a distance of the block (default 500 m, at most `MAX_CORRIDOR_M`), one road file, or the segments under one timber mark within it. Road sections and segments (`FTEN_ROAD_SECTION_LINES_SVW`, `FTEN_ROAD_SEGMENT_LINES_SVW`) are read inside the block grown by the distance, Freshwater Atlas streams within `STREAM_READ_M` of those roads and round the PSCIS records, and each road × stream intersection is one candidate (two on one stream within `SAME_CROSSING_M` are one place), with the stream's blue line key, its route measure (the line's downstream measure plus the distance along it: Freshwater Atlas lines run from downstream) and its mapped gradient over `GRADIENT_HALF_WINDOW_M` either side (the line's own elevations; a mapped figure, never a field one). All four PSCIS stages are read in the same area and folded into sites by the PSCIS site rule (below), each record's stream the nearest mapped stream within `PSCIS_STREAM_M` (PSCIS has no blue line key); a site joins the nearest mapped crossing within `PSCIS_MATCH_M` on the same stream, nearest pairs first, never across two streams. A site with no mapped crossing is a `pscis` candidate, and sites within 15 m on the same road that the rule kept apart are offered as one candidate the reviewer adds whole or site by site. Ministry bridges and major culverts within `STRUCTURE_MATCH_M`, Water Act approvals and notifications within `APPROVAL_NEAR_M` and TRIM roads within `TRIM_NEAR_M` are read round the candidates, and fish observations by the crossed stream's 50k watershed code (FISS has no blue line key either; its dashed `NEW_WATERSHED_CODE` is FWA's `WATERSHED_CODE_50K` with dashes, `fissWatershedCode`). Road section names, map labels, PSCIS road names and comments, FISS's source reference, an approval's works description, a structure's crossing and road names and every holder, client, consultant, crew and agency field are never asked for, and every record is cut to the reader's fields on arrival (`keepFields`); from PSCIS's free-text road tenure only file-id tokens are kept (`tenureTokens`). A layer that fails is recorded *Not read* with its reason, never as none; the read fails only when neither road layer reads. bcfishpass's modelled crossings are not read: its feature server offers no crossings and its download is a whole-province package, so a mapped crossing is computed from the two layers its model starts from.
- **Keys and aliases** (`crossings/key.ts`). A record keeps its own key; every id a source gives the place is an alias with its source and the day the source saw it: `pscis` (a crossing id), `mof-structure` (`CROSSING_SITE_ID`), `designer` (road and register row), `mapped` (road file and section × blue line key at its route measure), with `modelled` and `field` kept for later reads. A candidate goes onto the record that holds any of its aliases, else onto the nearest record the PSCIS site rule would join it with (*Add to …*, or *Add as its own*), gaining its aliases, evidence and sightings and any place, stream or structure the record lacked; nothing the record holds is replaced and its answers are never touched. A request letter quotes a crossing's PSCIS aliases and nothing else when it has any (`pscisIds`), so a letter never names another site's id.
- **The PSCIS site rule** (`crossings/pscisSites.ts`, `PSCIS_SITE_RULE`), as tested on the three districts' 4,232 assessments: records on two mapped streams are never one site; records of one survey day are one site only within 5 m; records of different days are one within 15 m, or within 30 m on one stream with route measures within 10 m; a join that would put two records of one day more than 5 m apart in one site is refused (the day guard), and records sharing a crossing id start as one site. Successive structure records at a site read as the same (type, and size within 15% and 5 cm), a likely replacement (a larger pipe or an open-bottom structure three or more years later) or a difference with no stated reason, which the candidate's note names. Change the distances only with a new run over the districts' records.
- **Evidence and what it offers.** Each record read is a `CrossingEvidence` with its kind, source, read date, the day it describes, its distance from the crossing and its kept fields (`values`); `kind` is an open word. A PSCIS barrier prints as PSCIS's assessment of a structure, never as a §56 finding; "no fish seen" on a visit and no observation recorded print as what they are, never as absence; a notification near a road prints as the §39(1) route used near there by someone, never as the forest road's notice (WSR §38(1) excludes §39(2)). What the evidence offers for a fact (`factOffers`) is derived from the record each time and never stored, and becomes an answer only on **Use**, with its basis kept as the answer's evidence (`applyCrossingOffer`): a change in and about a stream where a road crosses a mapped or assessed stream, the structure and a culvert's equivalent diameter, the mapped gradient, *Yes* to limb (a) where a fish the definition lists is recorded on the stream (never *No*), and the dating's candidate for `built-after-1995`.
- **Dating** (`crossings/dating.ts`). Sightings are dated records of whether the road and crossing were there: a PSCIS survey and TRIM's source date are added by the read (TRIM's flagged where it equals the date the line was admitted, which may be an update's), and the reviewer adds an air photo, a Wayback release, a Sentinel-2 or Landsat scene, a harvest record or a field visit, present, absent or unclear. The bracket is "first seen between" the latest sighting without it before the earliest with it; a sighting without it after it was first seen is shown as a contradiction. For FPPR §56(2) the candidate is *No* only when it was first seen before 15 June 1995, *Yes* only when it was not there on or after that day, else unknown, and it always asks whether the structure was replaced since.
- **The map** (`crossings/mapFeatures.ts`, `PlanMap.tsx`). The plan map draws each crossing with a place, for the schedule shown, coloured by its own standing (`CROSSING_COLOURS`), and each candidate of a current read that no record holds, smaller and faded; clicking either selects its block. The legend lists only the kinds drawn.
- **Counted, never answered.** Candidates read and not added are counted beside the block's question (its `stream-changes` and `crossings-listed` hints, and the section's summary); a crossing nobody listed is still not asked about. The printed summary gives each crossing's ids and dating bracket, and the assessment record carries its aliases, sightings and evidence; reads themselves are not printed.
- **Kept apart.** `crossingReads` is optional and parsed tolerantly: a plan without reads opens and writes exactly as before, a read that does not parse or names a block or schedule the plan no longer has is left out, and removing a block or schedule removes its reads. Reads, aliases, sightings and evidence stay out of `usePlanAnalysis`, the rule evaluation (until an offer is used) and every check's key.
- **Live runs.** `mappedRead.live.test.ts` (`CROSSINGS_LIVE=1`) read two made-up squares on 27 November 2026: one near the city with five PSCIS sites and no tenured road (23 requests, 1.3 s: five PSCIS-only candidates, two with no mapped stream within 30 m), and one on forest roads north-west of Prince George (7 sections, 7 segments, 53 stream lines and 4 PSCIS records; 17 requests, 1.4 s: three road × stream crossings, each joined to its PSCIS site, and one PSCIS site with no mapped stream). Every record came back with only the listed fields.

What is still left is in [Block planner: next steps](forestry-block-planning-next-steps.md): WSR §§41, 43 and 45 and the per-block exception signals, the province's fish habitat access model and bcfishpass's species access as evidence, air photo frames listed for the reviewer to mark, the imagery timeline's *Mark present / absent here*, and the field pack.

### Getting the records a review lacks

Most of what decides a harvested block's review is not public: the forest stewardship plan's amendment in force when the permit issued, the site plan, riparian classes, fish-stream determinations, a crossing's design, build date and maintainer, Water Act notices and terms, and contravention determinations. Review → **Records to get** (`src/pages/block-planning/acquire/`) lists what each block's and each crossing's review is still waiting on, says where to ask, drafts the request, tracks it and takes back what arrives as the reviewer's transcription. Review → **Forest stewardship plan** names the plan as far as public data can. The method and its trials are [Getting the records a harvested block's review lacks](research/acquiring-missing-records-2026-11-27.md).

- **The channel catalogue** (`packages/legal-rules/acquire/channels.json`, parsed by `acquire/channels.ts`). Each channel is an office or a role, never a person: who holds the record, the page its route starts at (each checked live on 27 November 2026), what to ask, the identifiers a request quotes, its fee, its time (`days`: 30 business days and a 30-day extension for a freedom of information request; null where nothing sets one) and the FOIPPA sections likely to cut what comes back. Where the law says something about a channel it is cited with a quote of the stored text, checked word for word by `acquire.test.ts` (FRPA s. 20.25(2)–(3)'s publication duty, s. 10's site plan, Forest Act s. 115's road permit, WSR s. 38(1)(b)'s officer's terms, FRPA s. 71 and s. 123(1)); FOIPPA is not stored and is named, not quoted. Each gap kind (`fsp-amendment`, `site-plan`, `riparian-class`, `small-streams`, `fish-stream-determination`, `crossing-design`, `crossing-build-date`, `crossing-maintainer`, `water-notice-terms`, `contravention`) tags the facts it answers (`pack:fact`, each fact once) and lists its channels, most direct first. `bctsPlans` lists BC Timber Sales' plans by FSP id with their own pages.
- **Gaps are derived, never stored** (`acquire/gaps.ts`). A tagged fact is a gap while it is unanswered and a rule that is not settled reads it, in the alternatives no answer has ruled out (a raised rule only through the conditions of an exception claimed on it). A gap is *Asked now* when the review asks one of its facts, else *May be needed* once an earlier answer says its rule applies. The FSP gap also waits on the plan and amendment the reviewer states. Ids are stable: `<kind>:<block>:<schedule>[:<crossing key>]`. BC Timber Sales' channel is offered only for a BCTS block and the holder's only for another's, from the tenure answered or the plan stated; when neither says, both.
- **Requests are drafts** (`acquire/requests.ts`, the pattern of *On this ground*'s `ground/requests.ts`): one letter per channel per block, covering every gap that channel can answer there, so a block's crossings go in one letter. A letter names the holder by role, quotes the identifiers the plan holds (the block's opening, licence, cutting permit and its issue date, timber mark, road permit and section, water file and FrontCounter BC tracking numbers, the stated plan and amendment; each crossing's road file, section and chainage, Freshwater Atlas stream key, place and PSCIS ids) and says what to ask and why. A letter to an office has a freedom of information version with the date range (the reviewer's, else ten years before the harvest to today) and a line inviting severing over refusal (FOIPPA s. 4(2)). Nothing is sent, and no name is written: an identifier is kept only when it holds a number (`identifierToken`), and a road's or stream's free-text name is never quoted. The letters are copied from the section, or saved as text from Save & export (**Records to request**).
- **Status** (`Plan.acquisition.requests`, `acquire/status.ts`): one record per gap and channel, a list of dated events (requested, time extended, received, partly received, refused, not held, withdrawn) with a reference and note. One click records an event on every gap a letter covers. A request is due its channel's days after it was requested, plus an extension's days for each recorded; business days skip weekends and British Columbia's statutory holidays, and the office's own count decides. A request whose gap an answer later settles is kept and shown *No longer needed*, never deleted.
- **Received records** (`Plan.acquisition.received`): a title, the channel it came through, the document's date, the day received, its reference, whether parts were severed and under what, and the values read from it, each the reviewer's transcription with optionally the question it bears on, its crossing, a page and a quote of at most 124 characters. The document itself is never kept. A value feeds nothing until the reviewer uses it (`acquire/received.ts`): as the answer to its question in the block's or the crossing's review (one click; a value the question cannot take is refused with the reason, and the answer's evidence names the record), as evidence for an exception claimed on a raised rule, or as the crossing's evidence of kind `received`. Each use is recorded on the value.
- **Identifiers** a request quotes are entered under *Identifiers a request quotes*, per block (`Plan.acquisition.ids`).
- **The forest stewardship plan** (`acquire/fsp.ts`, `FspPanel.tsx`), on the reviewer's click and for the session: the plans' units over the block through the ground screen's reader and catalogue rows (`readGroundLayer`, the `fsp-fdu` layer and its two instruments), or from a ground read already kept; each plan's amendments as the layer holds them, read by `FSP_ID` for every status with the same layer and fields, with the first held, any number the layer skips and the latest approved by a stated permit issue date (a candidate, never the answer); the opening's stocking standard regime ids from RESULTS (`RSLT_STANDARDS_UNIT_SVW`), to find in the plan's stocking standards, which names the plan and never its amendment; the BC Timber Sales page of a BCTS plan; and the Forest Operations Map for blocks since 2022 (its API gives `fspId` without a sign-in but sends no CORS headers, so it is a link). Every record is cut to its fields on arrival: no holder's or plan's name is read or kept. The plan, amendment and how it was identified that the reviewer states are kept with the block's identifiers; the amendment's text is a drafted request under *Records to get*.
- **Kept apart.** `acquisition` is optional and parsed tolerantly: a plan without it opens and writes exactly as before, and an item that does not read or names a block or schedule the plan no longer has is left out and counted in the notes on opening. Removing a block or schedule removes what was kept for it. It stays out of `usePlanAnalysis` and every check's key. The printed summary has a **Records asked for and received** section (identifiers and the stated plan, each request's status, dates and references, each received record with its transcribed values and where they were used) and the assessment record carries `acquisition` with each request's derived status; neither prints anything of a document beyond the reviewer's transcribed values. Nothing here says a block complies.

### One question, two instruments

Two instruments can ask the very same fact about the ground. A pack says so on the fact (`sameAs: "<pack id>:<fact id>"`, **Add an instrument** in `packages/legal-rules/FRAMEWORK.md`); a link is refused unless both facts take exactly the same answers (type, unit, bounds and choices, `checkFactLinks` in `contract.ts`, checked for the bundled library and on every install). Linked today: whether the block or its road is in a forest landscape area, which the permit pack asks under FRPA §3(0.1) and the forest operations pack under §2.35(1). A block review asks a linked question once, under the first release that asks it; the others show *Answered under …* (or *Asked under …*) with **Change**, and changing or clearing it there changes both (`review/linkedFacts.ts`). The answer is copied into each release's own fact set, so each release still evaluates and replays from its own facts alone, and the evaluator and the decision flowchart read nothing across packs. A copy is never written without the reviewer: where the releases already hold different answers (a review saved before the link) both are shown and neither is overwritten until the reviewer chooses one answer for both, and an answer held under one release only (a release added to the case later) is offered there with **Record it here too**.

## Real data from DataBC

Step 1's **Unit boundary and DataBC** builds the baseline from public records instead of the sample.

- **The unit.** **Read the order's unit** reads the order's own polygons from DataBC's legal planning objectives layer (`RMP_PLAN_LEGAL_POLY_SVW`, plan "Prince George Timber Supply Area (TSA) Legal Order - Landscape Biodiversity Objective Order - Extent", legalised 2004-10-20; `orderUnits.ts`) and joins those of the chosen district (`ORG_CODE` DPG, DJA or DVA) and sub-unit (`NDZ_NAME`, `NDZ_SUB`) into one area. The layer's attribute pairs are checked by name on every polygon, never by position. A sub-unit without a second name (McGregor Plateau, Wet Mountain, Northern Boreal Mountains) carries a single space in `NDZ_SUB`, not an empty value; until 24 November 2026 the reader asked for an empty one and found none of their polygons. It also names the order's landscape units (`UNIT_IDENT`) the sub-unit falls in, which Objectives A and B are set by. **Load a boundary** takes the analysis's own boundary as GeoJSON instead.
- **Past disturbances.** Consolidated cutblocks (the road designer's source, `bcHarvest.ts`; built mostly from RESULTS openings, which are not read again) and historical fire perimeters (WFS, box in `CQL_FILTER` longitude first), each with its source year (`baselineImport.ts`, in a worker). Reads start 20 years before the earlier of the first projection year and 2015, so the plan can be checked against the Board. Each part of a scattered unit is read by its own box, joined only where that adds little area; each record is kept once and named by its opening or fire number. Cutblocks are paged by `OBJECTID` (`readCutblockPages`): the service cuts pages short on its transfer limit (105, 488, even 0 of 1,000 records, each flagged as having more), and a reader that stopped at the first short page lost 134 of Prince George Moist Interior – Mountain's 2,739 cutblocks. A page the service takes too long over comes back from the gateway as 502 or 504 after two minutes; it is asked again at half the size (from about 08:00 UTC on 24 November 2026 every read of Prince George Moist Interior – Mountain failed this way at 1,000 records a page, and 500 came back in a minute). Reading again replaces what an earlier read added and keeps what was drawn or imported. The read is recorded on the plan (`area.baselineRead`: from which year, how far past the unit, when).
- **Patches at the boundary.** With a unit, **Whole patches** (the default) reads disturbances 5 km past it (`BASELINE_MARGIN_METRES`), builds patches from all of them, classes each by its whole size and counts only its young forest inside the unit (the engine's `countWithin`). **Clipped** cuts every patch at the boundary first. The choice is `area.patchExtent`; without a unit the engine runs exactly as before.

## Checking the method against the Board

It runs on request, says when the baseline was not read far enough back to hold 2015's young forest, and goes stale when the area or baseline changes. A difference tests today's records and the method together, never one of them: the Board used other inventories, and 2018 the revised 2017 guidance.

The check runs on the baseline's harvests alone. The Board's figures are the LLOWG's, which collects licensees' harvest, road and retention data, not fire perimeters, and leaving fires out brought the check closer in both years and both units tried. ## Explain a result and prioritize a field visit

Open **Explain the patch calculation** below the map. Pick a size class to see the young hectares divided by the reporting unit’s total young hectares, the target and the movement since the prior period. The contribution table names the input footprints behind each mapped patch. Their shared area counts once. A large current gap and a large move away from target can occur in different classes.

Under **Prioritize field measurements**, enter a plausible disturbance-date error in years. The worker changes one footprint at a time and recalculates both periods, including overlap and patch merging. It ranks cases where a date correction can reverse the class screen (whether any one class moved farther from its target, the test of releases to 1.2.0), identifies the smallest correction that does so, and shows changes in evidence availability and class measurements. Choose a range supported by the inventory. The tool has no preset accuracy estimate for VRI.

For a worked example choose **Landscape → Load Prince George sample**, **Proposed harvest**, map year **2031**, and a **±1 year** test. In this synthetic landscape Opening A and Harvest now can each reverse the flag. Opening A can move a current class share by about 66.67 percentage points. Harvest now can change the prior-period comparison while leaving the 2031 class shares unchanged. Opening B has no effect over the same range.

Use **Test a field measurement** for different lower and upper disturbance years or enter one observed year twice. Record the source. A missing input year supplied by the editor default is labelled in the comparison. The test assumes that geometry and all other dates stay fixed. Stand age from VRI needs a supported link to the date of a stand-replacing disturbance. Boundary uncertainty can be examined by redrawing or importing a surveyed footprint in a separate saved plan. The automated field-priority calculation covers dates only. Joint inventory errors and the relative likelihood of year estimates need additional evidence.

Both assessment exports include the displayed contribution table, source geometries and completed field comparisons for the active inputs. The scenarios are exploratory and leave the reviewer’s D.5 determinations independent. The historical benchmark covers all 25 Prince George district A/B units and six published C distributions. ## Spatial method and validation

Method `pg-2005-buffer-v1` takes the union of all qualifying footprints aged 0–20 inclusive in each period. Overlaps count once. Starting with disjoint polygon parts, it applies the 2005 guidance's five size-dependent stages. At each stage it groups eligible neighbours transitively within the stated join distance, dissolves buffered neighbours and erodes the result by the same distance. With a reporting unit and whole patches, the footprints are those read up to 5 km past the unit; every stage runs on them all, and a patch's young hectares inside the unit are what it contributes to its class and the denominator (`Project.countWithin`). A patch with none inside is left out. A dissolved group keeps its identity through subsequent stages, including when its eroded shape has multiple parts. Unjoined shapes retain their original geometry. A group's envelope area determines subsequent eligibility and final class. Its original young hectares contribute to the denominator, excluding the bridged land. These are modelling interpretations pending comparison with the original GIS outputs. Printed thresholds are applied literally. Areas in the gaps at 50–<51 and >100–<101 ha remain unclassified and are reported in the denominator. A zero denominator produces undefined percentages.

The change in absolute distance from the target compares one class across two periods; the trend rule adds the four classes' distances and compares the sums (**The young-forest trend**). The Board's Table 2 records 22.7% and 17.9% against 20% for the under-50 class in Fort St. James, Moist Interior–Mountain. That is a 0.6 percentage-point move closer. It tests the arithmetic only. The spatial comparison is the Board check above; the original inventories and the revised 2017 method would be needed to reproduce it exactly. Projected percentages cannot establish the legal trigger, the shortest practicable recovery or permission to harvest.

## Comparable implementations

Ontario’s Forest Management Planning Manual connects spatial harvest schedules to strategic analysis over short, medium and long horizons.
