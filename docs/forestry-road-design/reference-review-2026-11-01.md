## Switchbacks: calculation and display decisions

Use fitted centreline geometry throughout. A single vertex making an almost 180-degree
reversal is not a stable way to specify a circular return bend: the tangent length
`R * tan(deflection / 2)` becomes unbounded as the deflection approaches 180 degrees.
Two connected curves with adequate tangent room make the intended geometry explicit.
This agrees with the workflow in Softree's resource tutorial [S4], without copying
its implementation or treating its example dimensions as design rules.

For a circular arc, arc length is `R * angleRadians`. With constant inside formation
offset `w` and constant crossfall, the inside edge has radius `R - w`; its geometric
grade is `centrelineGrade * R / (R - w)`. If width or crossfall changes, or the inside
edge reaches the curve centre, that formula must be withheld. It is not a wheel-track
grade, vehicle traction model or permissible-grade rule. Sample spacing also limits
what extrema are observed. Label centreline and inside-edge values separately.

Inside daylight that reaches the curve centre signals a potential swept-section
overlap. Even positive inside radii do not prove that the two legs, their batters or
clearing footprints are disjoint. Existing average-end-area quantities use centreline
chainage; they are not a curvature-corrected swept-prism integration. Keep these
limits with the curve review and any exported review table.

### Why no automatic "BC switchback" preset is added here

The current official manual [S5] is **May 25, 2026**, 326 pages. The downloaded
file's SHA-256 is
`03ab8c6d2039c854e4c43f13358bfe838bd58a7b73a6d6a21f54a6deaf215db0`.
Its pp. 100/126 provide suggested grade/curve controls; pp. 131-132 provide
WB-19 additive widening by radius and deflection. Inside widening has a minimum
30 m outside transition and at least 70% widening at curve ends. Log overhang and
slip are excluded. These are project-selected criteria, not an automatic approval.

The current engine evaluates widening per curve. A continuous 180-degree maneuver
represented by two 90-degree curves must not be presented as a verified WB-19
maneuver simply because each curve has a table result. Its transition model also
needs explicit reconciliation before a sourced preset can claim to implement that
manual. `L²/(2R)` remains an identified approximation. Future work should model the
whole maneuver, apply taper requirements, preserve source/version information and
validate representative articulated vehicles independently.
