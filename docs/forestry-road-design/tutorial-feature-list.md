# DDC Access features for tutorial planning

## Part 1 · Work with one road

| Lesson | Features to cover | What the learner can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1. Open a project and choose a workspace](../../public/help/open-hillside/index.html) | Choose Ribbon, Guided map, or Engineering workbench. Switch interfaces while keeping the current road and station. Start a project, open a library example, or reopen a PGMaps `.road.json` file. Use browser autosave and save a portable project copy. | Open **Hillside access** on the synthetic training terrain and find the active road. |
| 2. Read the same station in three views | Use **Linked overview** to move between the plan (where the road goes), the profile (how it climbs), and the cross-section (how it fits across the hillside). Enter a station or select one on the drawing. In **3D terrain**, orbit or follow the station and show the cut and fill slopes. | At **0+200**, read the 7.3% road grade on the profile and the 2.03 m centreline cut in the section. The **?** beside the station controls opens the [illustrated guide and practice lesson](../../public/help/station-200/index.html). |
| 3. Choose terrain and add map context | Switch between training terrain, published BC LiDAR, and the regional elevation model. Import GeoTIFF elevation grids or LAS/LAZ point clouds. Inspect the source, resolution, vertical datum, and coverage. Draw a terrain profile, import GIS reference layers, and annotate the plan with pins, lines, areas, or sketches. | Check which ground surface supplies the road elevations and mark a location for field review. |
| 4. Create and edit a road | Sketch freehand, place pegs on the map, enter a bearing and distance, or import a line from GeoJSON, a zipped shapefile, KML, or KMZ. Load mapped roads in view, trace a visible road, or fit it to a road bench in LiDAR. Extend the road, move points, duplicate an alignment, and undo or redo edits. | Create a short spur, then duplicate it before changing the route. |
| 5. Shape the road in plan and profile | Set grade and radius limits. Fit horizontal curves, add spiral transitions, and lay out switchbacks. Edit vertical control points, grades, and curve lengths. Keep named profile alternatives on one horizontal alignment. | Change one bend or one design elevation and inspect the affected stations. |
| 6. Build the road cross-section | Set left and right formation widths, crossfall, ditches, cut and fill slopes, and surfacing. Preview and save templates, assign them by station range and side, and add components or layers. Apply curve widening, banking, turnouts, and landings. | Add a turnout and compare the section before, within, and after its taper. |

## Part 2 · Check the design and share it

| Lesson | Features to cover | What the learner can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7. Estimate earthworks, material haul, and cost | Read cut, fill, and surfacing quantities by station and for the whole road. Define stripping, material horizons, and volume factors. Use the mass-haul chart to see where excavation supplies fill. Assign borrow pits and waste sites, compare gravel sources and haul costs, and edit unit rates. | Explain where a road needs imported material, then trace one item in the preliminary cost estimate back to its quantity and rate. |
| 8. Review streams, drainage, and crossings | Draw or import streams, load BC stream and fish records, and review stream classes and riparian buffers. Examine catchments, flow paths, ponding, and cross-drain candidates. Compare runoff assumptions, enter culvert geometry and check cover, then record structures and costs in the crossing register. | Follow a stream from the map to its crossing record and identify what needs a field check. |
| 9. Check standards and compare alternatives | Set the job’s grade, radius, width, clearing, and haul criteria. Use **Ready to review**, model flags, and the filterable station table to find issues. Compare roads and named profiles by geometry, earthworks, and cost. Check how much of each road has terrain coverage before comparing totals. | Find a flagged station, inspect it on the drawing, and explain one trade-off between two alternatives. |
| 10. Prepare drawings and export results | Preview a PDF or HTML report, map PDF, block map sheet, or plot book. Configure sheets, labels, and issue stamps where available. Export station, section, engineering, or stakeout CSVs, stream records, GIS linework (GeoJSON or shapefile), and LandXML alignment or terrain files. Export an embedded terrain grid as an Esri ASCII raster. | Hand a reviewer a drawing, a station table, and a project file they can reopen. |

## Part 3 · Specialist workflows

| Topic | Features to cover | Example exercise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terrain preparation and survey | Combine overlapping grids or point clouds, retain or edit a triangulated surface, use breaklines, and add independent canopy or imagery backgrounds. Enter a survey traverse and side shots in the field book. | Combine two ground sources and inspect their overlap along a proposed road. |
| Route search and optimization | Search between endpoints using terrain and constraints, mark avoidance areas, and allow reviewed existing-road reuse. Optimize within a sketched corridor, optimize the vertical profile against controls, or refine plan and profile together. | Compare a searched route with the original sketch and locate the bend or grade that changed. |
| Cutblock access and road networks | Find or draw cutblocks, investigate historical access, and review dated road-condition, deactivation, and bridge evidence. Connect landings to forest service roads, compare network layouts and development stages, and adopt a proposed branch. | Connect two landing targets and compare how much new road each layout requires. |
| Harvesting reach | Map skidding distance from roads, identify areas outside reach, and assess suggested spurs and landing sites. Draw cable profiles (D-lines), examine skyline clearance and tension, and estimate reach in different directions from a landing. | Find an unserved part of a block and test a spur or cable landing for it. |
| Vehicle movement, timber haul, and full route cost | Check a logging truck’s swept path through a bend or junction. Choose a haul destination, orient the junction, and estimate loaded and return travel times. Compare connector construction, road rehabilitation, crossing costs, and delivered materials across routes. | Check whether the truck can make the turn, then see whether reusing an existing road changes the preferred cost option. |
| Personalize the workspace | Find commands and settings with **Find tools**, reorder ribbon groups, pin controls, change the road summary, resize panes, and save a named screen layout. | Save one layout for pegging and another for reviewing drawings. |

For the first recording, use the synthetic Hillside access example so the station readings match the existing lesson. Published LiDAR, basemaps, and live BC records depend on external services. The [road-design guides](README.md) cover data requirements and calculation limits for the specialist lessons.
