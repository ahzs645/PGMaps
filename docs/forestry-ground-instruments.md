# On this ground: the legal instruments mapped on a block

Step 5's **On this ground** section reads, on the reviewer's click, every legal instrument DataBC maps on the block shape under review, and shows for each one found: how much of the block it covers (or, for a line or point layer, how much lies inside the block and within a stated margin of it), what it can and cannot govern for four activities (harvest, replanting, roads, grazing), how it binds, whether its terms are public, what has already happened inside it nearby (precedent, with the public signals of an exception), and the documents to ask for. It sits beside **Crown grazing and reforestation** in `/dev/forestry/block-planning`.

It is a screen to plan the review, never a legal determination. Nothing in it says an instrument applies, that a block may or may not be cut, or that an exception is met; it answers no review fact. An instrument that is not mapped can still apply, and a layer that was not read says _Not read_, never _none_.

## The catalogue

One entry per kind of instrument. Each names its DataBC layer and the conditions that pick its records out of it, the statute and section it rests on, how it binds, a **reach** cell for each activity, where its terms live and whether they are public, and the review facts it informs (by pack and fact id). Each layer names the fields it keeps, how a record is named and identified, and where its effective date is, or that it has none.

A reach cell is one of:

- **Governs**: the instrument itself decides whether the activity happens there.
- **Conditions**: the activity can happen, under the instrument's conditions.
- **No reach**: the instrument, read from its text, sets nothing on the activity.
- **Unknown**: the answer turns on terms the reader does not have (an order's own text, a notice's terms, a general wildlife measure). The note names the document that decides.

A **context layer** (`context` on the layer) is read for what lies under a block but holds no instrument with reach cells: no instrument names it, the parser refuses one that does, and every other layer must hold at least one instrument. Its `context` says why it is listed and why it has no cells. The planner's _On this ground_ does not read it; the visual quality land checks do. Two are listed (version 1.2.0): First Nations treaty lands (`FNT_TREATY_LAND_SP`) and Indian reserves (`CLAB_INDIAN_RESERVES`), because the final agreements, the Acts that bring them into effect, the Indian Act and the federal reserve records were not read for the catalogue. A context layer gets a cell only as an instrument, from its primary text.

Every cell cites at least one provision and a source in `packages/legal-rules/catalogue/sources.json`; `groundInstruments.test.ts` fails otherwise, and fails if an instrument whose terms are not public says _No reach_ for anything. Binds is one of _Directly_ (the instrument regulates the activity), _Through the FSP_ (an objective set by government the forest stewardship plan must address, FRPA s. 5(1)(b)(i), and its holder carry out, s. 20.22), _By referral_ (decision-makers consider it; it does not itself regulate) or _Not at all_.

Change a cell only from the primary text, with its provision, and keep this guide's table in step.

### Why, instrument by instrument

- **Land Act §§15–17.** A §15 order reserves Crown land "from disposition under this Act" (s. 15(2)); a §16 withdrawal is "from disposition under this Act" (s. 16(1)); a §17 designation withdraws land "from disposition under this Act for any purpose that is not, in the opinion of the minister, compatible" with its use (s. 17(2)). A disposition is an instrument "by which an interest in Crown land is disposed of ... or by which the government divests itself of or creates an interest in Crown land" (s. 1), and the Act's dispositions are sales, leases, rights of way or easements, licences to occupy and fossil rights (s. 11(2)). The Crown land registry keeps out "an agreement to harvest Crown timber under the Forest Act", a forest service road and a Range Act grazing or hay licence or permit (s. 7(5)(b)–(d)). On that reading the withdrawal alone does not bar a Forest Act harvest, road or Range Act agreement. But each instrument can carry terms: a §15 order "may be subject to any terms and conditions" (s. 15(4)), and the minister may impose terms and conditions "on the use of land" withdrawn (s. 16(3)) or designated (s. 17(1.1)). Whether a term reaches timber harvesting, planting, roads or grazing is in the order or notice, not the layer, so every cell is _Unknown_ and names that document. This is a reading of the Act's text; no court decision on the point was found. The grazing review's own treatment of §17 is in [Crown grazing and reforestation](forestry-crown-grazing.md), **Land Act reserves and designated use areas**.
- **Leases, licences, rights of way.** Issued tenures only (`TENURE_STAGE` TENURE); an application or a notation is not one. An executed disposition binds the government (s. 8(3)) on the terms the minister imposed (s. 11(3)). Leased land is still Crown land, so its timber is Crown timber (Forest Act s. 1); the lease's terms decide what forest work on it needs. The Range Act's Crown range "does not include Crown land leased under the Land Act" (s. 1), so grazing on a lease is the lease's (_Governs_), and FPPR s. 44(3)(d), which needs a Range Act agreement, cannot rest on it. A licence or right of way is not a lease: its land stays Crown range.
- **Agricultural Land Reserve.** "Producing and harvesting timber, including engaging in silviculture and forest protection activities" is a designated farm use that may not be prohibited by a local government or first nation law (ALR Use Regulation ss. 4, 10(c)); the Act restricts non-farm use (ALC Act s. 20(1)); other enactments still apply (Regulation s. 2). Farming animals is a farm use (ALC Act s. 1). Roads are _Conditions_: removing soil or placing fill needs a permission, an approved notice or a regulation's permission (s. 20.3(1)); the regulation's permitted soil and fill uses (s. 35) list no new forest road, its permitted road works (s. 18) are within dedicated rights of way and forest service roads, and whether a new block road is part of the farm use of producing timber is not stated.
- **2006 Omineca ADA/SRA order.** As read for [Crown grazing and reforestation](forestry-crown-grazing.md): timber is managed only under Parts A, B and E; Part C.5's "No activities related to site preparation and artificial regeneration" binds a Part B harvest; C.4 minimizes permanent access. Its objectives manage timber and set nothing on grazing; Part II leaves range use plan schedules in effect for the year the order was made.
- **2004 Prince George TSA biodiversity order.** Objectives A (old forest), B (old interior forest) and C (young patch size distribution), subject to D; forest stewardship plans submitted after it took effect must be consistent with it (E). None sets anything on planting or grazing. The planner already measures it: Objective C in Compare and the landscape review, A and B in Old forest.
- **Other legal objectives and legal OGMAs.** Established by order under Land Act s. 93.4; the order decides. The planner holds rules for the two orders above only. A legal OGMA contributes to the Prince George order's A and B (D.1).
- **Ungulate winter ranges and wildlife habitat areas.** Established under GAR ss. 10 and 12 with general wildlife measures under s. 9(2). An authorized person who carries out primary forest activities (timber harvesting, silviculture, road work: FPPR s. 1) "must comply with each general wildlife measure ... that applies to the area" (FPPR s. 69); range practices must be consistent with them (RPPR s. 36(1)). `TIMBER_HARVEST_CODE`, as the BC Data Catalogue defines it, "contains information on timber harvest in the UWR polygon under FRPA. e.g., "NO HARVEST ZONE" or "CONDITIONAL HARVEST ZONE" - consult Order for General Wildlife Measures". A no harvest zone's harvest cell is _Governs_ and a conditional zone's _Conditions_, each naming the measures as the deciding text; the other activities are _Unknown_ until the measures are read. A record without a code (999 winter range records) is its own instrument with harvest _Unknown_.
- **Range Act agreements.** Active boundaries only. FRPA s. 48 (natural range barriers) and s. 51 (range developments need the minister's written authorization) condition harvest and roads; RPPR s. 44 requires an agreement holder to remove livestock where grazing "will cause significant interference with the establishment of a free growing stand"; FPPR s. 44(3)(d) lifts the free-growing duty only where the land is used exclusively for grazing or hay under a Range Act agreement. The agreement grants the grazing (Range Act s. 3), after an approved range use or stewardship plan (FRPA s. 32(1)).
- **Parks, ecological reserves, protected areas.** In a Class A, B or C park a natural resource "must not be granted, sold, removed, destroyed, damaged, disturbed or exploited except as authorized by a valid and subsisting park use permit"; in a recreation area, except as the minister approves (Park Act s. 9(1), (3), (6)); timber cut in a park is disposed of under the Forest Act (s. 15); a Schedule D park's range tenures continue under the Range Act within the animal unit months authorized at establishment (s. 30(5), (6)). An ecological reserve is "withdrawn and reserved from any further disposition", the Forest Act and Range Act named (Ecological Reserve Act s. 5(1), (2)), with use only by research or education permit (s. 5.1). A protected area is established by its own instrument (some records name a Park Act section; an Environment and Land Use Act order binds ministers' other powers, s. 7(2)), so every cell is _Unknown_.
- **Private land.** "Crown timber" is "timber on Crown land, or timber reserved to the government", and "private land" is land that is not Crown land (Forest Act s. 1). A Crown timber disposition does not reach a private parcel except timber reserved to the government, so the owner's title governs; Crown range is Crown land (Range Act s. 1). Private managed forest land is a different regime and is not assessed. The request goes to the district, never to an owner, and no owner is read.
- **Notations of interest.** The 2021 reserves policy (§5.5) makes a notation of interest "not an authorization under the Land Act, but rather a notation to identify a feature or concern", recorded so its holder is referred to on Land Act applications; it "does not preclude the acceptance and decision of Crown land applications". Its examples are trapline cabins, private moorage application-only areas, First Nation cultural features, historical artifacts and salt licks. It sets nothing itself on forest work, but what it marks may need care, so every cell is _Unknown_ and names its notice of establishment.
- **Applications.** A Tantalis record at stage APPLICATION. "A person does not acquire a right, vested or contingent, in Crown land, or a priority to Crown land by filing an application" (Land Act s. 8(2)); a disposition binds the government only once executed (s. 8(3), (4)); nothing obliges the government to consider an application or to grant it (s. 10(2)); an accepted application is published (s. 32(2)). So every cell is _No reach_: the row signals that a disposition may be decided here, and if one is issued the lease, licence or right of way row applies on its terms (s. 11(3)). A §15–17 designation or a notation at application stage is listed under its own row, which takes it first.
- **2006 Robson Valley ADA order.** Read from its enabling document (`robsonvalley_lrmp_luor_23nov2006.pdf`, four pages: the order's three and Map #6). It covers agriculture development areas in the Headwaters Forest District (Map #6, McBride area) and no settlement reserve areas; "The land use objectives apply to all agriculture development areas outside the boundaries of the McBride Community Forest in effect as of the date of this order" (I.ii). Its Parts A to E and II read word for word as the Omineca order's, Part D included ("No area based forest tenures will be established"), so its cells are the Omineca order's. It takes effect on publication in the Gazette (III) and is filed at the Southern Interior Forest Region office in Kamloops (V); its map "is a visual representation and is not for legal purposes". The grazing pack's ADA/SRA rules (`bc.crown-grazing`) would hold for it part for part, since the Parts match, but the pack is not applied to it: its source and its `ada-sra-applicable` fact name the Omineca order, and it would also need to ask whether the block is outside the McBride Community Forest as it stood on 21 November 2006, which the Omineca order has no counterpart for.
- **Mineral and placer tenures.** A recorded holder "may use, enter and occupy the surface of a claim or lease for the exploration and development or production of minerals" once any Mines Act permit is issued (Mineral Tenure Act s. 14(1), (2)). The holder does not own the timber: on request it gets a free use permit or an occupant licence to cut under the Forest Act (s. 14(3), (4)). A later disposition of surface rights "does not diminish the rights of the recorded holder" except as an order or agreement determines (s. 16(3)). Whether a Forest Act harvest or road is such a disposition is not stated, so every cell is _Unknown_; no court decision on the point was read. Coal tenures (Coal Act) are left out.
- **Water licences.** A licence entitles its holder to divert and use water and to build and operate the works (Water Sustainability Act s. 7(1)); anyone proposing to interfere with works must give the holder 180 days' written notice (s. 34). A primary forest activity, silviculture included (FPPR s. 1), must not damage a licensed waterworks (FPPR s. 60(1)) or put material harmful to human health in water diverted for human consumption (s. 59); in a community watershed harvest and roads keep 100 m upslope of such an intake unless sediment delivery will not increase (s. 60(2)), fertilizer is limited (s. 63) and water licensees get 48 hours' notice of road work (s. 84). Range practices follow RPPR s. 33. The layer is points: they are counted inside the block and within 100 m of it, the radius of s. 60(2), as a stated screen.
- **Woodlots, community forests, First Nations woodland licences and TFLs.** Each must give its holder "the exclusive right to harvest timber" on its Crown land for its term (Forest Act s. 45(1)(c) woodlots, s. 43.3(1)(c)(i) community forests, s. 43.55(1)(c)(i) First Nations woodland licences, s. 35(1)(e) TFLs), so harvest is _Governs_. That right gives way to a BC Timber Sales licence only where the holder released rights under a BCTS disposition agreement (s. 22.2(5)); a TFL must also make timber available to others in an amount the minister directs (s. 35(1)(h)). A woodlot's Schedule A is the holder's own private land or a reserve (s. 45(1)(b)(i)). The licence document and its plan decide roads, and whether its terms restrict grazing (its Crown land stays Crown range), so those cells are _Unknown_. Managed licences are told apart by the file's letter (W, K, N) as the old forest land base reads them; TFLs are read by each footprint part's hull, as there.
- **Recreation sites and trails.** Established by the minister's order, which may set an objective (FRPA s. 56(1), (3)); a site, trail or interpretive forest is a resource feature (FPPR s. 1), and a primary forest activity "does not damage or render ineffective a resource feature" (FPPR s. 70(1)); the minister may restrict or prohibit a non-recreational use of one by order, with notice posted (FRPA s. 58(1)(a)(ii), (3)); building a trail or facility needs written authorization (s. 57(1)). Only the establishing order and any s. 58 order say what more applies, so the cells are _Unknown_. Recreation reserves and trail reserves are not established under s. 56 and are left out. Trails are lines: their length inside the block and within 50 m of it is measured, a stated screen.
- **Visual quality.** A visual quality objective is one continued under FRPA s. 181, established for a scenic area under GAR s. 7(2), or a visual quality class continued under GAR s. 17 (FPPR s. 1); its categories are extents of alteration "resulting from the size, shape and location of cutblocks and roads" seen from a significant public viewpoint (FPPR s. 1.1). A forest stewardship plan must specify results or strategies for it (FRPA s. 5(1)(b)(ii)). So harvest and roads are _Conditions_ and planting and grazing _No reach_. The inventory's `REC_EVQO_CODE` (P, R, PR, M, MM) is the established objective; a polygon made known as a scenic area (`REC_MADE_KNOWN_CODE` Y) with none is its own row: FPPR s. 9.2 sets an objective by its visual sensitivity class only for a scenic area established on or before 24 October 2002, which the layer cannot date, so its harvest and roads cells are _Unknown_. `REC_EVQO_CODE` also holds `SCO` on 1,006 polygons; the catalogue does not define it, so it prints as given under the scenic area row.
- **Federal critical habitat.** On land in a province that is not federal land, "No person shall destroy any part of the critical habitat of a listed endangered species or a listed threatened species" (SARA s. 61(1)) "applies only to the portions of the critical habitat that the Governor in Council may, on the recommendation of the Minister, by order, specify" (s. 61(2)); an emergency order can prohibit activities that may adversely affect the species and its habitat (s. 80(1), (4)(c)(ii)); on federal land the protection runs through s. 58(1), (4), (5). The posted layer identifies habitat; it regulates nothing on provincial Crown land without such an order, so every cell is _Unknown_ and names the order and the recovery strategy. No order for southern mountain caribou was looked for in this read; the research note's search found no s. 61 order. Provincial measures (winter ranges, habitat areas, an agreement under s. 11) are separate rows. One habitat polygon can span 500,000 ha, so its share is the block's, not the habitat's.
- **Community watersheds.** FPPR ss. 59–63 and 84 apply to primary forest activities in a community watershed (FPPR s. 1 includes those designated under GAR s. 8): no material harmful to human health in diverted water, no harvest or road within 100 m upslope of a licensed waterworks unless it will not increase sediment delivery to the intake (s. 60(2)), roads kept back from identified springs (s. 62), fertilizer limits (s. 63), 48 hours' notice to water licensees before road construction (s. 84). Range practices follow RPPR ss. 33–35.

## General wildlife measures

Where the order behind a found ungulate winter range or wildlife habitat area has been read, the order answers the four activities for the unit or zone the feature is in, in place of the catalogue's _Unknown_ cells. The orders read are data (`packages/legal-rules/ground/wildlife-measures.json`); `src/lib/legal/wildlifeMeasures.ts` parses them by hand and `ground/wildlifeMeasures.ts` answers a feature from them. Both are DOM- and network-free.

**What is recorded for an order.** Its id, the ranges or areas it establishes (`UWR_NUMBER` or the WHA `TAG`), the published PDF, the decision date and the date it took effect, how it was made, its zones and, for each measure that bears on harvest, replanting (silviculture, stocking, site preparation, brushing, pesticides), roads (construction, deactivation, access) or grazing (range use and developments): the measure's number as the order prints it, a paraphrase, a quote of under 125 characters, the zones it applies to, and whether it _governs_ the activity (decides whether it happens: "No primary forest activities", "Timber harvesting and road construction must not occur") or sets _conditions_ on it. Measures about fire, recreation, mineral exploration or tourism that touch none of the four are left out. The order's exemption and approval clauses are their own list, each with who decides and whether it is part of the order or only its appendix or supplement.

**Zones.** An order names its zones by unit (`UWR_UNIT_NUMBER` as the layer gives it, keyed by the range's number where one order establishes several), by harvest zone, or as the whole order. The grizzly bear order for WHA 7-001 draws its Core and Management zones on its map only; the screen takes DataBC's no harvest records as Core and its conditional records as Management, and says so. A unit an order does not name stays _Unknown_ ("names no zone for unit …; the order's map decides"), with harvest still answered by the layer's code.

**How a cell is answered.** For each activity, the measures applying to the feature's zones that bear on it: _Governs_ if any governs, else _Conditions_, the note listing each measure with its paraphrase and quote, the cites naming the order's measures (each order is a `regional_order` in `catalogue/sources.json`) and FPPR s. 69 for a general wildlife measure (RPPR s. 36(1) for grazing). Where no measure for those zones bears on the activity the cell is _No reach_: the order, as read, sets nothing on it; other law still applies. Features of one instrument whose units the order answers alike share a row in the panel and the printed summary; the record keeps each row under `byOrder`.

**Orders made under the Forest Practices Code.** U7-002 and U7-013 (2003) were made under the Operational and Site Planning Regulation s. 69 as management objectives and continued by FRPA ss. 180–181. Their measures are objectives, never general wildlife measures (the parser refuses a Code order with a GWM): a forest stewardship plan carries them out through its results or strategies (FRPA s. 5(1)(b)(ii), (1.1)) and is amended for them (FPPR s. 28.1(1)(d)), so their rows bind _Through the FSP_, even where the objective's words are a prohibition ("no commercial forest harvesting").

**Exceptions to request.** For every order read that a found feature is in, the panel and the printed summary list the order's own clauses for the zones found (GWM 2(b)–(d) of U-7-003, the guyline tiebacks of U-7-019, U7-013's variance by the statutory decision maker, the "unless an exemption is approved" clauses of WHA 7-003 and U-7-009, and so on) with who decides, and the statutory route beside them: FPPR s. 92(1) (the minister responsible for the Wildlife Act may exempt a person from a general wildlife measure if its intent will be achieved or compliance is not practicable) and RPPR s. 36(3) (a range holder's alternative), or, for a Code order, the forest stewardship plan. An appendix's guidance is marked as not part of the order. Nothing says an exception applies or would be granted.

**Not read, and not published.** An order found on the block but not yet read keeps the catalogue's cells, harvest answered by the layer's `TIMBER_HARVEST_CODE` as before, every _Unknown_ naming the order and its PDF. A range or area no list names says its order decides. Data-sensitive habitat areas have no published order: GAR s. 10(3) keeps their location out of the order and gives it to affected Forest Act and Range Act holders in writing. They stay _Unknown_, cite s. 10(3), and get a drafted request under **Documents to request**.

### Adding an order

1. Find it in the ministry's approved UWR or WHA table and download the order, and any amendment, to a scratch folder; never commit the PDF.
2. Read the whole order. In `wildlife-measures.json`, record its zones as the order names them, checked against the layer's `UWR_UNIT_NUMBER` values for that range (a unit the layer holds that falls in no zone will read "names no zone"), then each measure bearing on one of the four activities: the number the order prints, a paraphrase, a quote under 125 characters copied from the order, the zones, and governs or conditions. Record every exemption, approval or "does not apply" clause with who decides; mark an appendix's or supplement's guidance `inOrder: false`.
3. A Code order (made under the Operational and Site Planning Regulation, continued by FRPA s. 181) has objectives, never GWMs.
4. Take its range or tags out of `unread`, add the order to `catalogue/sources.json` as a `regional_order` with the same URL, run `npx vitest run src/lib/legal src/pages/block-planning/ground`, and update the table above.

## How the read works

Each layer is read once, by the footprint's own parts (`INTERSECTS` with each part's outer ring, by POST, through `readGroundLayer` and `wfsReads.ts`), never its bounding box, with the CQL of every instrument it holds joined by OR (a context layer is read whole, and only by the land checks). Its records are then shared out to the catalogue's instruments by the same conditions, the first instrument a record meets taking it, and clipped to the footprint in BC Albers. An instrument's covered area dissolves its records, so ground two of them share is counted once. Overlaps under 1 m² are the overlay's rounding and are left out.

A line or point layer (`geometry` in the catalogue) is read against the footprint grown by its stated margin (`near`), plus 2 m for the ring's rounding: a line's length and a point's count are measured inside the footprint and within the margin, a length two records share counted once. TFLs are asked by each part's convex hull, because DataBC times out intersecting a detailed part with a licence of tens of thousands of vertices; the clip is exact either way. Precedent is read inside area instruments only.

**No holder's name.** Each layer's fields are listed narrowly and never include a holder's, client's, owner's, applicant's or organisation's name, an address or an IDIR user. DataBC's GeoServer returns a layer's mandatory attributes whatever `propertyName` asks: the range tenure layer sends `CLIENT_NAME` and `CLIENT_NUMBER`, the legal planning layer `GIS_CHANGE_PERSON`, RESULTS its `*_WHO_*` IDIR users, and Forest Tenures cut blocks `CLIENT_NAME` (checked 24 November 2026). The mineral layer's `OWNER_NAME` and `CLAIM_NAME`, the water licence layer's `PRIMARY_LICENSEE_NAME` and address lines, the TFL layer's `LICENCEE` and an ALR application's `APPLICANT_NAME` are never asked for. So every record is cut down to the listed fields as it arrives (`keepFields`), and nothing else is held, stored or printed; `groundRead.test.ts` pins it.

A layer DataBC did not answer makes every instrument it holds _Not read_, with the reason, grouped by the reason in the panel and the summary. A layer holding more records against the footprint than the reader keeps is marked incomplete.

## Precedent

For each instrument found, what RESULTS records has already happened inside it near the block. Openings (`WHSE_FOREST_VEGETATION.RSLT_OPENING_SVW`) are read once by the hull of each part of the footprint grown by `PRECEDENT_RADIUS_METRES`; an opening belongs to an instrument when it shares ground with one of its features clipped to that grown footprint, and was harvested (`DISTURBANCE_START_DATE`, else the first denudation's completion) on or after that feature's effective date where its layer gives one. Where the layer has no date every opening inside counts, and the panel says where the date is (GATOR for Tantalis, the RAN file for range). An opening with no harvest date recorded is kept and counted apart. Openings harvested before the date are counted, not listed.

Standards units (`RSLT_STANDARDS_UNIT_SVW`), planting results (`RSLT_PLANTING_SVW`) and site preparation and planting activities (`RSLT_ACTIVITY_TREATMENT_SVW`, `SILV_BASE_CODE` SP and PL) are joined by `OPENING_ID` in batches. For each opening the record keeps its id, map label, forest file id and file type code, cutting permit and block, category and status codes, gross area, harvest and denudations, site preparation, planting (date, area, trees by species with a species' seedlots added together), and each standards unit's regeneration and free-growing dates and declarations, minimum and target stocking and preferred species. Never `CLIENT_NAME` or `CLIENT_NUMBER` (a client can be a private person), never an IDIR user.

Each instrument's precedent prints as one line: _N openings within 5 km harvested since <date>; M planted; K declared free growing; J with no planting recorded_, then the public signals below where there are any. A join that failed prints _planting not read_ or _free growing not read_, never a count of none. Codes print as RESULTS gives them.

## Documents to request

For each instrument found whose terms are not public, a drafted request: who holds the terms, what to ask for (the catalogue's `terms.ask`), the identifiers read from each feature (Crown lands file, document and interest numbers; RAN file and map block; PID; protected area name) and the channel (FrontCounter BC for Land Act files, the natural resource district for range plans and private parcels, BC Parks for a protected area). It is text to copy; nothing is sent. A private parcel's request goes to the district, never to its owner.

## The record

The read is kept with the plan in `Plan.groundReads`, one per block and schedule, keyed to the schedule's resolved shape and its footprint signature; the catalogue version it was read under is kept with it. When the shape changes the read shows as out of date until it is read again, and `saveGroundRead` refuses a read for other ground. Removing a block or schedule removes its reads. A plan without reads opens as before; a read that does not parse, or names a block or schedule the plan no longer has, is left out, never repaired. It never enters `usePlanAnalysis`, the rule evaluation or a check's key. The assessment record carries each read with whether it is current, the catalogue's reach for what was found, each instrument's precedent line and the requests; the printed summary shows the matrix with each cell's provisions, what was not read, the precedent and the requests.

## Not built

- **The terms themselves.** No order, notice, lease or range plan is read except the wildlife orders under **General wildlife measures**; every other _Unknown_ names the document.
- **The rest of the region's wildlife orders.** The Mackenzie and Vanderhoof orders and the other regions' orders reaching the Omineca are listed as not yet read, and the data-sensitive habitat areas cannot be read from anything published. Amendments are read only where listed (none of the orders read shows an amendment on the ministry's table). Zones drawn only on an order's map (WHA 7-001's Core and Management) are taken from the layer's harvest code. A measure is matched to the feature's unit, never to where in the unit the block falls, and a measure tied to a distance (500 m from a no harvest unit, 2 km from a Caribou High unit) or to whether caribou are present is quoted, not measured.
- **Other instruments.** Fisheries sensitive watersheds, temperature sensitive streams, First Nations reserves and treaty lands (listed only as context layers, with no reach cells), private managed forest land, coal tenures (Coal Act), water approvals and notations (`WLS_WATER_APPROVALS_SVW`, `WLS_WATER_NOTATION_SV`), recreation reserves and trail reserves (not established under FRPA s. 56), wildlife management areas, Forest Act designated and special purpose areas, and the BC Data Centre's occurrences are not in the catalogue.
- **The other signals.** RESULTS reserves (`RSLT_FOREST_COVER_RESERVE_SVW`, the FPPR s. 1(2)(f) reserves), opening categories other than SPEX that move an obligation (`FTFSM`, `FFTITSL`, `FFTFLTC`), the RESULTS "declared to the extent practicable" indicator (not among the public columns) and the ALC's decisions themselves are not read. A water licence, mineral title or recreation feature has no precedent: precedent is read inside area instruments only, and a water licence is a point.
- **Orders behind the federal and recreation rows.** No order under SARA s. 58(4), 61(2) or 80, and no FRPA s. 56 or 58 order, is read; each row names it as the deciding document.
- **Effective dates where the layer has none.** Tantalis, range tenures, the ALR and ParcelMap carry none; GATOR, the RAN file, the Commission's records and the title do, and the reviewer records them in the grazing review's evidence.
- **Case law.** The Land Act §§15–17 cells rest on the Act's text alone; no court decision on whether a Land Act withdrawal or its terms reach a Forest Act harvest was found.
